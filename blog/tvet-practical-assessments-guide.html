What Happens During a TVET Practical Assessment?

NCHSM Editorial Team · Updated 26 October 2026

Practical assessments give learners an opportunity to demonstrate what they can do—not only what they can recall. In its report dated 31 October 2025, Nakuru College of Health Sciences and Management described TVET-CDACC practical assessments involving students from several programmes at partner health facilities, followed by further practice in the college skills laboratory.

This guide explains the assessment approach described in the official NCHSM report and offers general preparation tips. Exact assessment formats and requirements depend on the programme and current examining-body instructions.

What did the college report?

The report describes students in Perioperative Theatre Technology, Health Records and IT, Community Health & Development, and Science Laboratory Technology taking part in practical assessments at partner facilities including Nakuru Nursing Home, Bondeni Sub-County Hospital and Rhonda Hospital. It states that lecturers conducted assessments under TVET-CDACC supervision and guidelines.

According to the report, learners applied classroom knowledge in practical settings, then continued with additional sessions in the skills laboratory before theory assessments later in the month. This illustrates how observation in a practice setting and structured skills-lab work can complement each other.

How to prepare before assessment day

  • Read the instructions: confirm the assessment date, venue, required identification, dress code and any materials you are permitted to bring.
  • Review the relevant outcomes: use your unit notes, practical checklists and lecturer guidance to identify the skills and knowledge being assessed.
  • Practise the sequence: rehearse the relevant task in the approved learning environment, following the procedures taught for your course.
  • Prepare professionally: arrive on time, follow placement rules, communicate respectfully and observe confidentiality.
  • Prioritise safety: use required protective equipment, follow infection-prevention procedures where relevant, and ask your supervisor if an instruction or hazard is unclear.
  • Bring only permitted items: do not assume that phones, notes or personal equipment can be used during an assessment.

During a practical assessment

Listen carefully to the instructions and make sure you understand the task before beginning. Work methodically, explain your reasoning when requested, and follow the safety and professional standards taught for that activity. If you make a mistake, do not hide it or improvise beyond your competence; follow the assessor's instructions and report any safety concern immediately.

Assessment is not only about speed. Accuracy, safe practice, communication, preparation and the ability to follow the relevant procedure may all matter, depending on the competency being assessed. Always use the rubric and instructions supplied for your own course rather than assuming every programme is assessed in the same way.

Use feedback to improve

After the assessment, ask which competencies you demonstrated well and which need more practice. Convert feedback into a short action plan: name the skill, identify the specific step that needs work, arrange supervised practice and check progress with your lecturer. Repeated, guided practice is more useful than simply repeating a task without understanding the feedback.

Practical assessment is not a substitute for professional authorisation

Completing an assessment is one part of a training programme. It does not by itself guarantee employment, registration or a licence to practise a regulated profession. Students should confirm programme completion requirements and any additional requirements with their college, examining body and relevant professional regulator.

Source and scope: The event details in this article come from NCHSM's published report on TVET-CDACC practical assessments dated 31 October 2025. The preparation tips are general educational guidance; follow the current instructions for your programme and assessment.
