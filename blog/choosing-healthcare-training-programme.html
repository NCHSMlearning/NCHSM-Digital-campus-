How to Choose a Healthcare Training Programme in Kenya

Choosing a healthcare course is a decision about the work you hope to do, the training you need, and the learning environment in which you can develop safe professional practice. Use this checklist to compare options before you apply.

1. Start with the role you want to pursue

Healthcare includes many different roles. Nursing, community health, health records and information, laboratory work and other health-related disciplines have different responsibilities and training pathways. Read the description of the role itself, not just the course title. Consider the people you want to serve, the kind of work you are interested in and the skills the role requires.

2. Confirm entry requirements before applying

Check the current entry requirements for the exact programme and intake. Requirements can differ by course and may change. Confirm required academic certificates, identification documents, application steps, deadlines and any additional conditions directly with the institution. Do not rely solely on an old poster, forwarded message or social-media comment.

3. Verify recognition and professional requirements

For regulated health professions, identify the relevant regulator and verify the requirements that apply to the qualification you intend to pursue. Ask the institution which qualification it awards, what approval or accreditation applies to that specific programme, and what graduates must do before they can practise. Do not assume every health-related course leads to the same registration or scope of practice.

4. Ask how practical learning is organised

Healthcare education requires more than reading notes. Ask how skills practice, supervised clinical or field learning, assessment and feedback fit into the course. Find out what support is available when a learner needs more practice. Practical learning should emphasise patient safety, professional conduct, confidentiality, infection prevention, communication and working within the limits of one's competence.

5. Understand the full cost and schedule

Request the current official fee structure and clarify what it includes. Ask about payment dates, examination charges, uniforms, equipment, transport for placements and other costs that may apply. Confirm the expected timetable, duration, attendance requirements and placement arrangements before making a financial commitment.

6. Check student support and learning resources

Ask how learners access lecturers, academic guidance, libraries or learning materials, student welfare support and information about assessments. If you visit a campus, observe whether information is clear and whether staff can answer questions about the programme. A clear process for raising concerns is an important part of a supportive learning environment.

7. Prepare a careful application

  1. Choose the exact programme and intake.
  2. Read the institution's current instructions and eligibility requirements.
  3. Prepare accurate copies of the requested documents.
  4. Use the institution's official application channel and keep a copy of your submission.
  5. Ask the official admissions office for clarification when an instruction is unclear.
  6. Keep receipts and written communication related to your application.

Questions worth asking before you enrol

  • What is the exact name and awarding body of the qualification?
  • Which current entry requirements apply to my intake?
  • What supervised practical learning is included?
  • What additional costs should I budget for?
  • Which regulator or professional body should I consult about registration?
  • Who can I contact for written clarification?
Important: This article is general application guidance, not a substitute for official admission instructions or advice from a professional regulator. Requirements vary by programme and may change. Always confirm current details with the institution and relevant regulator.
